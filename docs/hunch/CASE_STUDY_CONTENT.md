# Hunch

## Turning everyday food into something worth discovering.

Hunch is a mobile food-discovery and collection experience that lets people identify the food they encounter, uncover what makes it unique, and grow their collection one Hunch at a time.

**Role**  
Product Designer

**Timeline**  
Aug 2026 to Sep 2026

**Type**  
Freelance Client Project

**Scope**  
Product Design · Brand Direction · Design System · Prototyping · Usability Testing · Developer Handoff


# Turning a simple idea into a complete product experience.

Hunch began with a simple concept: **“Pokémon Go for food.”** Users photograph food they encounter, identify what they have found, and gradually build a collection of discoveries.

Starting from the product PRD, I shaped the experience end-to-end, structuring the information architecture and core flows, defining the interaction and visual language, building a reusable design system, prototyping key journeys, and preparing the experience for development.

The goal was not simply to build a food-recognition tool, but to turn identification into an experience people would want to repeat.


# From a feature list to a connected product.

The initial brief defined the main features, but they still needed to work together as one coherent experience.

I structured Hunch around a simple recurring loop:

**Discover → Hunch → Identify → Collect → Explore**

This became the foundation for the product architecture, connecting onboarding, camera-based identification, the FoodDex, personal collections, progression, profile, and premium features.

For first-time users, the experience was designed to move from a lightweight introduction into an interactive first Hunch, allowing them to learn the product through use rather than through lengthy explanations.


# Teaching Hunch by doing.

Instead of explaining every feature in detail through onboarding alone, the first-use experience combines a short product introduction with a dedicated guided tutorial for the core Hunch flow.

A five-step tutorial introduces the interaction upfront, taking users through the Hunch process with clear step-by-step guidance. Users move through the tutorial using **Next**, with the option to **Skip** and continue independently.

## Making identification feel like discovery

Taking a photo is the central interaction in Hunch, so the experience needed to feel more meaningful than a standard image-recognition flow.

The journey moves through distinct states, from capturing and processing a food to successful identification, unsuccessful recognition, and food-bank recommendations, while keeping feedback lightweight and easy to follow.

Processing states use Hunch’s cloudy visual language to create anticipation before the food becomes fully identified.

## Turning discoveries into progression

Identification is only the beginning.

Every saved discovery contributes to a growing FoodDex and personal collection, supported by rarity, EXP, levels, and badges.

Together, these systems turn individual food captures into visible progress and give users more reason to continue exploring.


# A visual system built around curiosity and the diversity of food.

The identity began with the meaning of a **hunch**: an intuitive feeling before something becomes fully known.

That idea shaped a visual language that moves between uncertainty and clarity. Cloudy imagery represents the moment before a discovery is identified, while food becomes clear and explicit once it is known.

The two dots in the Hunch wordmark resemble a pair of glancing eyes, representing curiosity, awareness, and the instinct to look toward the next discovery.

## Curiosity before clarity

Uncertainty is expressed through soft, cloudy textures and obscured food imagery.

Once a food is discovered, the interface shifts toward clearer imagery and more explicit information, visually reinforcing the transition from a hunch into something known.

## Food brings the color

Rather than relying on a single dominant brand color, Hunch uses a flexible palette that embraces the natural visual diversity of food.

Food already varies dramatically in color, shape, texture, surface, and presentation. The visual system was designed to make use of that richness instead of forcing every discovery into the same fixed treatment.

**The interface provides the structure.  
The food provides the color.**

## Expressive content, restrained interface

Because the food itself can already be colorful and visually complex, the surrounding interface was intentionally kept minimal.

A restrained UI allows food imagery to become the focal point instead of competing with decorative interface elements.

Although Hunch borrows collecting and progression mechanics from games, its playfulness comes through discovery, rarity, EXP, badges, color, and the foods themselves rather than through a heavily game-styled interface.

This balance keeps the product playful while maintaining a clean, contemporary experience.


# Building a system, not just a successful flow.

The experience needed to remain understandable even when a Hunch did not follow the ideal path.

I designed reusable patterns across states including successful discoveries, repeat captures, non-food results, foods unavailable in the Food Bank, permission restrictions, weekly Hunch limits, premium features, badge unlocks, and level-up moments.

Rather than treating each state as an isolated screen, shared patterns were used for feedback, confirmation, navigation, and system messaging.

This helped keep the experience predictable while allowing new states and server-driven content to be added without breaking the overall visual and interaction language.


# Testing whether users could understand and repeat the core flow.

The interactive prototype was tested with **8 participants** across three stages: initial onboarding, a guided first Hunch, and a later repeat Hunch without contextual guidance.

## 8 / 8

Completed the guided first Hunch successfully.

## 8 / 8

Completed the repeat Hunch without guidance.

## 6.4 / 7

Average ease-of-use rating for the unguided repeat flow.

## The interaction worked. The product message needed more clarity.

Although participants were able to learn and repeat the core interaction successfully, their interpretation of Hunch after onboarding varied considerably.

Some understood it as a food journal, food-ordering app, calorie tool, or simple food identifier rather than a discovery-and-collection experience.

This revealed a distinction between **understanding how to use Hunch** and **understanding what Hunch is ultimately for**.

The onboarding was therefore refocused around three clearer actions:

**Find it → Hunch it → Keep it**

with stronger emphasis on the core idea of:

**food discovery → identification → collection**

After completing the guided first-use tutorial, all 8 participants rated their understanding of how to use Hunch at **7/7**.

The unguided repeat flow was also completed with a **52% lower median completion time** than the guided first-use flow.

*The first-use flow included additional tutorial guidance, so this timing comparison is treated as a repeated-use observation rather than a controlled efficiency measure.*


# From concept to a reusable product system.

The final delivery brought together Hunch’s product structure, visual identity, reusable components, interaction states, prototype, and implementation-ready assets into a consistent mobile experience.

The design system established shared patterns for navigation, buttons, fields, cards, feedback, discovery states, progression, and system messaging so that screens did not need to be designed as isolated outputs.

Key product behaviours and edge cases were also documented to reduce ambiguity during implementation and help the experience remain consistent as it moved into development.


# A hunch worth finding.

Hunch evolved from an initial product brief into a complete food-discovery experience with its own interaction model, visual identity, collection system, progression mechanics, and reusable product foundation.

The final experience turns a simple camera interaction into a recurring loop of curiosity, discovery, and collection, giving each Hunch somewhere to go beyond the moment it is captured.
